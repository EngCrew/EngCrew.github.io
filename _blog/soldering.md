Good soldering is mostly about heat going to the right place. Here's what makes the difference.

## Keep the tip clean and tinned

Wipe the tip on brass wool or a damp sponge and keep a thin coat of solder on it. A dirty, oxidised tip won't transfer heat.

## Heat the joint, not the solder

Touch the iron to the pad and the lead together for a second or two, then feed solder into the joint. It should flow toward the heat.

## Know what a good joint looks like

A good through-hole joint is smooth and shiny with a small cone shape around the lead. Dull, lumpy or balled-up solder usually means not enough heat.

## For surface mount, flux is your friend

Tack one pad first, align the part, then solder the rest. Extra flux helps solder flow, and solder wick cleans up any bridges between pins.

## Stay safe

Work with ventilation or a fume extractor, use safety glasses, and wash your hands afterward, especially with leaded solder.
